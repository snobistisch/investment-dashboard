Picking is a screen. Balancing is a matrix. They are not the same job.

This tool does not forecast returns, because the research says nobody can forecast them well enough for it to help. It does the two things that are measurable: it removes assets that fail a hard screen, and it weights what survives so that no single name and no single cluster decides the outcome alone. Bitcoin is the benchmark and is never a position — every number on this page is measured relative to it.

01

The objective decides the mathematics, and this objective is unusual

Most portfolio tools minimise volatility. That is the correct answer to a question this book is not asking. The stated objective here is to beat bitcoin, with a book that is allowed to be risky. Minimising volatility against that objective is a well-solved wrong problem: the least volatile assets in this universe are the most bitcoin-like, so weighting on absolute volatility converges on bitcoin's return minus costs.

So every statistic here is active — measured against the benchmark rather than against zero:

active returni,t = log ri,t − log rBTC,t
active volatilityi = σ(active returni) · √365
active correlationi,j = corr( active returni , active returnj )

That changes what the numbers mean. Active volatility is how far a name can travel away from bitcoin in a year — against this objective it is the resource, not the danger. A name with almost no active volatility is a bitcoin tracker with extra steps and cannot beat it. And active correlation answers the question the book has never been able to ask: are these two names the same deviation from bitcoin, rather than the useless one, do they both go up when the market goes up. In crypto everything goes up when the market goes up. That is not information.

Why there is no expected-return term anywhere in the weighting:

DeMiguel, Garlappi and Uppal tested fourteen optimisation models against simply splitting capital equally, across seven datasets. None beat equal weighting consistently out of sample, and the estimation window they compute as necessary for mean-variance to win is around 3,000 months for 25 assets and 6,000 for 50 — two to five centuries. The Kelly criterion has the same disease in a different coat: research on practical implementation finds a hundred trades insufficient, a thousand insufficient, and roughly ten thousand as the point where full Kelly earns its theoretical advantage.

Expected return is the hardest input to estimate and the one every optimiser is most sensitive to. Volatility and correlation are estimable from a year of daily closes, and this repository has already measured them. So the weighting uses only what can be measured, and the judgement lives entirely in the screen — where it is visible, arguable, and cannot silently size a position.

03

How many bets are actually in here

The repository has claimed for months that seven tabs are three bets. This is the first time it is computed rather than asserted — and computed on active correlation, so shared beta to the market does not count as similarity.

Names are grouped by average linkage on the distance √(0.5·(1−ρ)), cut where active correlation exceeds 0.6. A pair with too little overlapping history is treated as maximally distant, so a recent listing sits in its own cluster rather than being merged on no evidence.

Weights are equal active-risk contribution, not equal money:

Σij = ρij · σi · σj   (active covariance)
solve w ≥ 0, Σw = 1 such that wi·(Σw)i is equal for all i
then apply the per-name and per-cluster ceilings and renormalise

Equal money gives a quiet name and a violent one the same say in the outcome, which is not balance — it is arithmetic mistaken for balance. Equal active-risk contribution means every position has the same shot at deciding whether this book beats bitcoin. That is the honest reading of "balanced" for a benchmark-relative objective.

05

Does the machinery earn its complexity?

The same names, weighted three ways. If the clustering and the risk parity do not beat splitting the money equally, they are decoration and this table says so.

Read this as a risk statement, not a return statement. Every figure is computed from measured volatility and correlation. None of it says which portfolio will earn more — that depends on returns nobody here can forecast. What it does say is how concentrated each version's risk is, and whether the largest position quietly decides the result.

06

What this tool cannot do, stated before you use it

Horizon

One year of history is one regime

Every volatility and correlation here is measured over a single year that contains one large drawdown. Correlations rise in crashes — that is their defining inconvenience — so the diversification measured in calm is an overestimate of the diversification available when it matters.

Rebalancing

These weights drift the day after you set them

The stated approach is to rebalance by hand when the market moves. Drift bands are shown per position for that reason: a weight that has drifted past its band is where the book stopped being the book you chose. Nothing here assumes a schedule.

Costs

Spread and slippage are not modelled

The screen removes the least liquid names, which is the blunt version of a cost model. It is not a cost model. At the volumes at the bottom of this universe, the spread is a meaningful fraction of a year's edge.

Selection

The lens is a judgement and stays one

The weighting is measured; the picking is not. Research EV rests on subjective probabilities, momentum on the assumption that the last year says something about the next, the fee lens on the assumption that revenue reaches holders. Switch between them and watch the book change — that disagreement is the most useful thing on this page.

No forecast

There is no expected return anywhere

Deliberately. Every number is a risk number. A tool that told you what these assets will return would be lying with more decimals.

Benchmark

Beating bitcoin is not the same as making money

Everything is relative. A book that beats bitcoin by twenty points in a year bitcoin falls by half has done its job and lost you money. That is what choosing a benchmark means and it is worth deciding on purpose.

Sources & method

Volatility and correlation are computed from one year of daily USD closes per asset, fetched from the CoinGecko public API by scripts/fetch-risk-rating.ts and stored in public/data/crypto-history.json. Active statistics, clustering and the correlation matrix are computed by scripts/build-portfolio.ts and written into this page as a generated block — the page fetches nothing at runtime. Screen inputs (float, FDV/cap, volume, fees) and the selection lenses come from the Assets tab's own ranking table; the risk rating R comes from public/data/risk-rating.json.

Method sources: DeMiguel, Garlappi & Uppal, Optimal Versus Naive Diversification, Review of Financial Studies 22(5), 2009 · Busseti, Ryu & Boyd, Risk-Constrained Kelly Gambling, 2016 · López de Prado, Building Diversified Portfolios that Outperform Out of Sample, 2016
Weighting is equal active-risk contribution with per-name and per-cluster ceilings. No expected-return input is used anywhere in the weighting.
Not investment advice. This is a research tool that reorganises measured risk statistics. It does not forecast returns, has no track record, and its selection lenses rest on assumptions stated on this page. Verify every figure before acting on it.
