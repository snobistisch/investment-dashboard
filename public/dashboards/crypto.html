01

Three groups, and the dividing line is who still has tokens left to sell

The structural arguments for crypto have not changed and are mostly right. Stablecoins do settle real volume. Tokenised treasuries are operational rather than a pitch deck. Perpetual DEXs genuinely take share from centralised exchanges. None of that has been worth owning. Over the last twelve months twenty-five of the twenty-seven assets with a full year of history fell — nineteen of them by 60% or more — while the arguments themselves got stronger, which is the definition of a thesis that is right about the world and wrong about the price.

So the useful question is not which narrative is true. It is what each price already assumes, and how many tokens are still queued up behind it. That second half is where crypto differs from equities: a company cannot print 78% more shares on a published schedule, and several of the assets here can and will. Grouping by that — rather than by narrative category — produces three tiers that behave completely differently, and the grouping is mechanical rather than editorial: it comes straight from fully diluted valuation over circulating market cap.

The tier boundaries are the ones the source research proposed for itself. Applying them to live supply data rather than to remembered supply data moves a lot of names, in both directions.

02

The benchmark is bitcoin, and almost nothing clears it

Crypto research has a habit of measuring itself against zero. Every token in this universe is a high-beta expression of one factor — global liquidity — and they move together in exactly the conditions where diversification is supposed to help. In that world the question "is this positive expected value in dollars?" is close to meaningless, because in a liquidity upcycle almost everything is. The question that decides anything is whether it beats the simplest, most liquid, most owned thing in the asset class.

So bitcoin is in the ranking as a row rather than in a footnote. On the scenario probabilities in §5 it carries an expected +18.5% per year over three years with the tightest outcome distribution of any asset here. Nine of the other twenty-nine beat that on raw expected value. One beats it once you divide by dispersion.

That result is not a bitcoin-maximalist argument and it is not an argument against owning anything else. It is a statement about what the alternatives have to earn. A token needs roughly double bitcoin's expected return to compensate for double its dispersion, and on these numbers only Ethereum comes close to doing that.

What survives the hurdle test

Three reasons to own something else

A structurally different cash flow. Ethereum's staking yield and Aave's fee capture are not bitcoin exposure with extra steps — they are claims on activity that exists whether or not the price rises. This is the only category where the argument is genuinely independent.

A drawdown large enough to change the arithmetic. Aave at $1.4bn and 96% float after a 71% fall, Celestia at $294m with 81% float — these are not cheap because the market is wrong, they are cheap because the market gave up. That is still a different bet from bitcoin.

Option value that bitcoin cannot express. Nockchain at a $28m capitalisation is a call on verifiable compute becoming a market at all. It ranks fifth on expected value and twenty-fourth of thirty on expected value per unit of dispersion, which is precisely what an option looks like when you measure it honestly.

What does not

The expensive middle

Tokens whose only claim is beta. Optimism, Lido, The Graph and Arbitrum are all governance claims on ecosystems whose economics do not reach the holder. They carry bitcoin's downside with a fraction of its liquidity and none of its monetary argument.

Winners that already won. Zcash is up 1,186% in twelve months and Hyperliquid 29% — the only two. Both are quality assets and both now price a great deal of good news. They rank 24th and 25th of thirty here, and neither ranking is a criticism of the protocol.

Products whose token is not the product. Ondo and Ethena both run real businesses that a token holder has no claim on. The source research is right about this and it is the one place where its "avoid" verdict survives contact with live data intact.

03

The float rule is right, and it points at different tokens than it used to

The strongest idea in the source research is a rule: do not buy a token where most of the eventual supply has not been issued yet, because the unlock schedule is a guaranteed seller that has nothing to do with whether the protocol succeeds. That is correct, it is unusually falsifiable for a crypto argument, and it is the kind of thing that separates analysis from narrative.

It also has a shelf life, because float moves. A token that was 12% circulating eighteen months ago is not 12% circulating now — the unlocks happened, the price absorbed them, and the overhang the rule was warning about is in the past. Checked against live supply data, the rule reverses on most of the names it was originally aimed at.

The contradiction worth stating plainly. The source research sets a rule — avoid tokens under roughly 30% float with FDV more than 3× circulating capitalisation — and then makes Hyperliquid its #3 conviction pick (22%, 4.5×) and Lighter its #8 (25%, 4.0×). Those are the only two names in the entire universe that fail the rule other than Pearl, which it does correctly reject. Either the rule has exceptions worth naming, or the picks do not survive it. This dashboard takes the second reading and prices the dilution into the bear cases; a reader who takes the first should raise both base cases and re-rank.

04

Verifiable compute is real technology and a 2029 investment

The most interesting claim in the source research is that computation itself becomes a tradable, auditable commodity — that zero-knowledge proofs and trusted hardware turn "trust the API provider" into "verify the output," and that this is what finally makes AI × crypto infrastructure rather than narrative. The technology assessment is sound. The timing conclusion is the one worth arguing with, and the research itself half-makes the argument before backing away from it.

Approach A

Zero-knowledge proofs

Cryptographic security, full public verifiability, no hardware trust assumption. The cost is latency and money: minutes of proving time and roughly $0.01–$1.00 per inference at current efficiency. Production-grade for rollup proving, not for real-time inference.

NOCK · RISC Zero, Succinct, =nil; — private
Approach B

Trusted execution environments

Microsecond latency and near-zero cost, at the price of trusting Intel or AMD silicon not to have a flaw or a back door. In production in every major cloud today. The pragmatic answer, and the one that is hardest to sell to an audience that came for trustlessness.

SCRT · Octra — limited float and data
Approach C

Fully homomorphic encryption

Compute directly on encrypted data. Still four orders of magnitude slower than plaintext. Genuinely transformative if the overhead falls; a research programme until it does.

Zama — pre-token, not investable

05

Thirty assets ranked by expected value, with the arithmetic shown

A composite score out of 100 hides the two things that decide an investment: how big the outcomes are and how likely each one is. This ranking replaces the score with an explicit three-scenario expected value per token. Every probability below is my judgement and is visible in the table — disagree with a number and you can recompute the ranking yourself, which is the entire point of showing it this way.

Method

For each asset, three scenarios over a three-year horizon to August 2029, with probabilities summing to 1 and a total dollar return in each. There is no earnings multiple to lean on for most of these, so the scenarios are set directly against a price path: what the token is worth if the thesis compounds, if it merely persists, and if it fails.

EV = Σ pᵢ · rᵢ
σ = √( Σ pᵢ (rᵢ − EV)² )
EVann = (1 + EV)^⅓ − 1

Bull — the thesis compounds and a liquidity upcycle arrives. Base — the protocol persists, adoption grinds, the multiple does not re-rate. Bear — the category does not matter, or the unlock schedule wins. For Tier C the bear case explicitly carries the dilution arithmetic from §3.

What this method cannot do

It is not a forecast and it has no track record. The probabilities are calibrated against nothing except my own reading of each situation. The honest claim is narrower than a prediction: this makes a judgement auditable, comparable across thirty assets, and falsifiable line by line.

Correlation is not modelled, and here that matters more than anywhere. These are not thirty independent bets — they are one factor with thirty tickers. In the bear case they do not fall independently; they fall together, which is exactly when a portfolio needs them not to. EV ranks assets; it does not size a book.

It is mechanically kind to drawdowns. A 90% fall raises expected return arithmetically if the asset survives — and in crypto, unlike equities, "down 90%" is often terminal rather than cheap. The ranking cannot tell those apart. Float quality and revenue do some of that work; judgement does the rest, and judgement is what is on trial here.

Position & moatThe only asset in this report that clears the bitcoin hurdle on a risk-adjusted basis, and it does so narrowly — 0.80 against 0.77. The argument is the one the source research makes and makes well: roughly half of global stablecoin supply settles here, most tokenised real-world assets are issued here, about a third of supply is staked and earns a yield that exists independently of price. The objection that actually bitesNot demand — value accrual. Ethereum spent five years successfully moving activity onto layer twos and making data cheap, and it worked: fees collapsed, which is excellent for users and ambiguous for holders. The bear case is not that adoption stalls, it is that adoption keeps compounding while the asset captures a shrinking share of it. A settlement layer that settles everything for almost nothing is a public good, not a cash flow. My 25%-weighted bear at $1,000 is that argument winning.

What would settle itTwo observable series, both quarterly. Net issuance — whether staking rewards outrun fee burn — tells you directly whether the asset is being diluted by its own security budget. And the share of stablecoin and RWA value that stays on mainnet rather than migrating to alternative settlement layers. If the first stays negative and the second holds above roughly half, the fundamentals-versus-price gap is real. If issuance runs positive while the RWA share drifts below 40%, this is a value trap with excellent infrastructure metrics, and the ranking above is wrong at the top.

07

What breaks the thesis — seven ways, each with a falsification trigger

A risk without a trigger is a disclaimer. Each of these carries a specific, observable event that would tell you the risk is materialising while there is still time to act on it — that is the only property that makes a risk list useful.

1 · It is one factor, and it is global liquidity

Every asset here is a levered claim on the same thing. Correlation with equities sits in the 0.6–0.8 range and rises toward 1 in exactly the drawdowns where diversification is supposed to pay. A thirty-name ranking looks like a portfolio and is a single position; the July 2026 drawdown is the calibration anchor for what that costs.Trigger: a 20%+ equity drawdown in which the median token here falls further than bitcoin — confirming there is no idiosyncratic support anywhere in the list.

2 · Bitcoin simply wins

The most likely way this whole exercise turns out to be wasted effort is not that crypto fails, but that bitcoin absorbs the category's entire risk budget. Institutional access exists for BTC and, at a stretch, ETH; it does not exist for the other twenty-eight. On this report's own numbers only one asset beats bitcoin risk-adjusted.Trigger: bitcoin dominance holding above 60% through a full liquidity upcycle — the alt-season that does not arrive is the falsification.

3 · Value accrual never reaches the token

The recurring pattern across six years: protocol usage compounds, token price does not. Chainlink's usage has grown every year since 2020 and LINK is 83% below its 2021 high. Governance rights are not cash flows, and several of the largest positions in this universe are governance rights with a story attached.Trigger: Uniswap's activated fee switch failing to produce a durable re-rating within four quarters — the cleanest natural experiment available, on the largest sample.

4 · Unlocks decide outcomes regardless of quality

The float rule in §3 assumes dilution is priced gradually. If instead each cliff produces a step down irrespective of fundamentals, then Tier C is uninvestable at any price and Tier B is worth less than modelled — and the whole idea of buying quality early is broken.Trigger: Lighter's Q1 2027 cliff producing a sustained decline while buyback volume is rising. That is the specific, dated test.

5 · The AI × crypto layer never finds a buyer

Verifiable compute is technically real and commercially unproven. If no external party is paying for proofs by 2029, the category was a narrative and Nockchain, Pearl and most of the compute tier were options on nothing.Trigger: no verifiable-compute network reporting $50m+ of annual external revenue by 2029 — and, sooner, Nockchain's first external paying customer, which has still not happened.

6 · Privacy is regulated out of the listed market

Zcash carries an $8.2bn capitalisation that depends on remaining accessible through tier-one venues. Delisting does not break the protocol — the shielded pool works either way — but it breaks the investment case for anyone who needs a regulated exit.Trigger: delisting from Binance or Coinbase, or two consecutive quarters of declining shielded-pool share, which would remove the usage evidence the thesis rests on.

7 · This ranking is biased toward things that already fell

Stated as a risk because it is one. Expected value mechanically rewards a low base, and in crypto a 90% drawdown is more often terminal than cheap. The Graph is down 85% and ranks 26th; Aave is down 71% and ranks first — the distinction rests on float quality and business durability, which is judgement, not data.Trigger: structural rather than observable. It is a reason to read the drawdown column and the float column together, never separately.

08

Five claims that have to be true — and what would change each one

These are not a stacked bull case. H1 and H2 pull against each other: if settlement value accrues cleanly to Ethereum, the application layer captures less of it. Which one you back determines which half of §5 you should be reading.

H1

The 2026 drawdown is a de-rating, not a verdict

The claim the entire ranking rests on. If a 69% median decline reflects liquidity leaving a category whose usage held up, the top of §5 is a discount. If it reflects the market correctly concluding that most of these protocols will never accrue value to their tokens, the ranking is systematically upside-down.

ConfidenceModerate
What would update itProtocol revenue and active-address series against price, quarterly. Usage holding while price falls supports the de-rating reading. Usage falling with price says the market is simply right, and the correct response is fewer names, not cheaper ones.
H2

Settlement is the durable layer, and it is Ethereum

Roughly half of stablecoin supply and most tokenised real-world assets settle on Ethereum, about a third of supply is staked, and the security budget is paid in the asset itself. This is the best-evidenced claim in the report and the reason ETH is the only name that clears the bitcoin hurdle.

ConfidenceModerate–high
What would update itNet issuance turning persistently positive — staking rewards outrunning fee burn — or the mainnet share of stablecoin and RWA value drifting below 40% as issuance spreads across chains. Either makes this a public good rather than an asset.
H3

Float, not narrative, explains most three-year returns

The organising rule of this dashboard. If it holds, the tier structure in §1 does more work than any thesis in it, and the correct screen is mechanical: buy float-settled survivors, avoid vesting calendars, ignore the story.

ConfidenceModerate–high
What would update itTier C outperforming Tier A over the next eight quarters. Hyperliquid is the live test and it is a fair one: the best business in the report sitting in the worst float tier is exactly the case that separates the rule from a superstition.
H4

Verifiable compute is a 2029 market, not a 2026 one

Zero-knowledge proving is production-grade for rollups and one to two orders of magnitude too slow and expensive for real-time inference. The source research says this plainly and then still sizes the category as though the timing were near. Treat these as options with long expiries or not at all.

ConfidenceHigh
What would update itProving cost per inference falling below roughly $0.01, or any network publishing verified external revenue for proofs. The second is the cleaner signal and would move Nockchain's bull probability materially.
H5

Privacy demand is structural, and already partly paid for

The shielded-pool growth from 11% to 30% of supply is real evidence of real usage, and it is the strongest adoption datapoint in the report. The open question is not whether the demand exists but whether a 1,186% twelve-month move has already collected on it.

ConfidenceLow — stated as an open question
What would update itShielded-pool share continuing to climb through a falling price would confirm the usage is not merely reflexive with the trade. The two moving together is the outcome that says this was a flow story wearing an adoption story.

09

Where to point due diligence next — mapped to which claim you back

Not a buy list. The ranking in §5 orders assets; it does not build a portfolio, and the correlation problem in risk 1 means the whole list is one bet wearing thirty tickers. What to diligence depends on which of the five claims in §8 you actually believe, and the three routes below imply genuinely different books — including one that concludes you should own less of this than you already do.

If you back H2 · Settlement is durable

Two assets and a hurdle

BTC · ETH
context: SOL as the beta expression

The conclusion the numbers actually support. Ethereum is the only asset that clears the bitcoin hurdle risk-adjusted, and it does so by 0.03. Diligence net issuance and mainnet RWA share — two series that settle H2 without reference to price.

If you back H1 + H3 · De-rating, float rules

Float-settled survivors

AAVE · LINK · SKY · UNI
smaller: AKT, TIA, PYTH

Buy the drawdown only where there is no vesting calendar left to fight. Diligence fee capture, not usage: every one of these has users and the question is whether the holder is paid. Uniswap's live fee switch is the cleanest evidence available.

If you back H4 · Compute is a later market

Options, sized as options

NOCK · AKT
avoid: PRL · not investable: RISC Zero, Zama

Nockchain ranks fifth on expected value and twenty-fourth of thirty on dispersion-adjusted return — own it only at a size that can go to zero. Diligence one thing: whether an external customer has paid for a proof. Nothing else in the category matters yet.

The one cross-cutting view I hold with reasonable confidence: the structural arguments in the source research are mostly right and mostly not investable through these tokens. Stablecoins are settling real volume, tokenised treasuries are operational, perpetual DEXs are taking share — and the assets attached to those facts fell by a median of 69% while the facts got stronger. That is not a market failing to notice. It is a market pricing the difference between a protocol working and a token holder being paid for it, which is the distinction the source research identifies as the central lesson of the category and then does not apply to its own top five.
