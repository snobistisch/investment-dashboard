Picking is a screen. Balancing is a matrix. They are not the same job.

This tool turns explicitly subjective return scenarios into a screen; it does not claim that those scenarios are forecasts. It removes assets whose stated edge fails evidence, cost or robustness gates, then weights what survives so that no single name and no single cluster decides the outcome alone. Bitcoin is the benchmark and is never a position — every comparison on this page is measured relative to it.

01

The objective decides the mathematics, and this objective is unusual

Most portfolio tools minimise volatility. That is the correct answer to a question this book is not asking. The stated objective here is to beat bitcoin, with a book that is allowed to be risky. Minimising volatility against that objective is a well-solved wrong problem: the least volatile assets in this universe are the most bitcoin-like, so weighting on absolute volatility converges on bitcoin's return minus costs.

So every statistic here is active — measured against the benchmark rather than against zero:

active returni,t = log ri,t − log rBTC,t
active volatilityi = σ(active returni) · √365
active correlationi,j = corr( active returni , active returnj )

That changes what the numbers mean. Active volatility is how far a name can travel away from bitcoin in a year. Active correlation asks whether two names are the same deviation from bitcoin, rather than whether both rise with the market. It is still a noisy estimate. The current all-pair, liquid-pair and bitcoin-stress figures are generated from the same refreshed closes and printed below.

Why there is no expected-return term anywhere in the weighting:

DeMiguel, Garlappi and Uppal tested fourteen optimisation models against simply splitting capital equally, across seven datasets. None beat equal weighting consistently out of sample, and the estimation window they compute as necessary for mean-variance to win is around 3,000 months for 25 assets and 6,000 for 50 — two to five centuries. The Kelly criterion has the same disease in a different coat: research on practical implementation finds a hundred trades insufficient, a thousand insufficient, and roughly ten thousand as the point where full Kelly earns its theoretical advantage.

Expected return is the hardest input to estimate and the one every optimiser is most sensitive to. Volatility and correlation are estimable from a year of daily closes, and this repository has already measured them. So the weighting uses only what can be measured, and the judgement lives entirely in the screen — where it is visible, arguable, and cannot silently size a position.

02

The screen: hard exclusions with a stated reason, not a score

A score lets a good number hide a fatal one. These are gates: fail one and the asset is out, with the reason printed next to it. A raw positive EV is no longer enough: the edge must also survive your cost estimate and a visible shift from the asset’s bull probability to its bear probability.

terminal EVi = Σs pi,s·ri,s
net edgei = EVi − EVBTC − round-trip costs
robust edgei = net edgei − δp·(rbull−rbear) > 0

Each return leg runs from the current reference price to a fixed terminal USD target over the published-year scenario horizon. The table annualises expected terminal wealth for comparison; the cost and probability gates are applied to terminal edge, before annualisation.

03

How many bets are actually in here

The repository has claimed for months that seven tabs are three bets. This is the first time it is computed rather than asserted — and computed on active correlation, so shared beta to the market does not count as similarity.

Names are grouped by average linkage on the distance √(0.5·(1−ρ)), cut where active correlation exceeds 0.6. Thirty-six assets have enough overlap to enter the calculation. CAP, ARX and PRL do not; they are unclustered and cannot enter the builder. Missing evidence is no longer presented as a distinct bet.

Equal weight is the pilot default. Equal active-risk contribution remains available as a comparison:

Σij = ρij · σi · σj   (active covariance)
solve w ≥ 0, Σw = 1 such that wi·(Σw)i is equal for all i
then apply the per-name and per-cluster ceilings and renormalise

The covariance estimate covers one regime and has no out-of-sample record. Complexity therefore has to earn its place. Start with 1/N; switch only when the comparison below shows a material concentration benefit you understand.

05

Does the machinery earn its complexity?

The same names, weighted three ways. If active-risk parity does not materially improve risk concentration over 1/N, it is decoration and the simpler allocation remains the pilot.

Read this as a risk statement, not a return statement. Every figure is computed from measured volatility and correlation. None of it says which portfolio will earn more — that depends on returns nobody here can forecast. What it does say is how concentrated each version's risk is, and whether the largest position quietly decides the result.

06

What this tool cannot do, stated before you use it

Horizon

One year of history is one regime

The generated cluster section compares active correlation across all measured pairs, liquid pairs and the worst 20% of bitcoin days. Diversification can shrink when bitcoin is falling. None of these estimates has been tested out of sample.

Costs

The cost buffer is an input, not an executable quote

The round-trip buffer covers fees, spread, slippage and a future exit as one conservative deduction from terminal edge. CoinGecko volume is global. Replace the buffer with an estimate from the exact venue and order book before freezing a decision.

Selection

The EV gate is still a judgement

Every surviving name has positive scenario EV versus bitcoin, but the probabilities are subjective and have no track record. Passing the gate means “diligence candidate”, not “known edge”.

No forecast in weights

Expected return selects; it does not size

Scenario EV is a visible subjective judgement. Volatility and correlation determine weights. The separation prevents an uncertain return estimate from quietly creating a large position.

Benchmark

Beating bitcoin is not the same as making money

Everything is relative. A book that beats bitcoin by twenty points in a year bitcoin falls by half has done its job and lost you money. That is what choosing a benchmark means and it is worth deciding on purpose.

Sources & method

Volatility and correlation are computed from one year of daily USD closes per asset, fetched from the CoinGecko public API by scripts/fetch-risk-rating.ts and stored in public/data/crypto-history.json. Current price, float, FDV and volume come from public/data/crypto-market.json; probabilities and fixed terminal targets come from public/data/crypto-scenarios.json. Active statistics, scenario repricing, clustering and the correlation matrix are computed by scripts/build-portfolio.ts and written into this page as a generated block — the page fetches nothing at runtime. The risk rating R comes from public/data/risk-rating.json.

Method sources: DeMiguel, Garlappi & Uppal, Optimal Versus Naive Diversification, Review of Financial Studies 22(5), 2009 · Busseti, Ryu & Boyd, Risk-Constrained Kelly Gambling, 2016 · López de Prado, Building Diversified Portfolios that Outperform Out of Sample, 2016
Equal weight is the pilot default; equal active-risk contribution remains a comparison. Both apply per-name and per-cluster ceilings. Expected return selects candidates but never sizes them.
Not investment advice. This is a research tool that reorganises measured risk statistics. It has no track record, and its scenario-EV gate rests on subjective assumptions stated on the Assets tab. Verify every figure before acting on it.
