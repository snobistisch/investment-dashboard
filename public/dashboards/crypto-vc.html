Crypto-VC returns are not public. No IRR, no DPI, no TVPI — every online tier list, including the one this note was built to check, is a judgement about reputation and deal access, not a measurement of performance. What is publicly verifiable is who was in which round, when, and how much it raised. This section is that database: 5,868 funding rounds scraped and parsed, with two funds — Haun Ventures and Paradigm — profiled in full, and the wider VC landscape assessed against the same evidence standard.

01

What is actually verifiable about a VC's quality

Every crypto-VC ranking — including the tier list this research was commissioned to check — is built on reputation and perceived deal access, because the number that would settle it, realised return, is not disclosed by any fund on any list. That is not a gap this research can close. What it can do is replace "reputation" with things that are actually observable: who leads a round versus who merely joins it, how a fund's pace moved through the 2022–2023 drawdown, which other funds keep showing up in the same deals, and which portfolio companies were realised through an acquisition with a public price.

Two funds are profiled at that level of detail below — Haun Ventures and Paradigm — chosen because they anchor opposite ends of the question this research keeps returning to: Haun is the fund with the clearest realised exits (Bridge→Stripe, BVNK→Mastercard) and no listed tier at all; Paradigm is the highest-tier fund on the list and, on the data, the one that leads nearly half its rounds and kept investing through the deepest part of the 2023 bear market.

02

Where this came from, and what broke along the way

The obvious sources are closed. DefiLlama's raises endpoint now returns 402 — paid tier. Crunchbase requires a key. RootData returns a 500 without one. What remained open was crypto-fundraising.info, a WordPress-based database with 6,407 individually addressable funding-round pages and 8,478 fund pages, none of it behind a login.

The fund pages themselves are not usable as a source. Each one renders only its ten largest rounds — the page's own source code names the variable topten — and every pagination parameter redirects back to page one. The only route to a fund's complete portfolio is the reverse one: fetch every round in the database and index it by investor afterward.

The first attempt at that failed silently. Fetching 6,407 pages with eight concurrent connections triggered Cloudflare's bot challenge on 5,079 of them — pages that came back as a "Just a moment…" interstitial rather than content. Every file existed and none were empty, so a naive completeness check would have called the run successful. It was not; a content check caught it. The corrected run used three concurrent connections, real browser headers, and a referrer, and recovered to 92% coverage before this pass was stopped to keep it within budget.

Per round, the parser extracts: project name (from the page's <title>, not the truncated body text), date, round type, raise amount, and the investor list split into lead versus other using the page's own newrised_investors lead markup — a distinction the first pass of this database missed entirely and which is the single most informative field in it.

04

The tier list separates funds that write the same deals

Coinbase Ventures is the single most frequent co-investor for both funds — 46% of Haun's rounds, 35% of Paradigm's. That is not a property of either fund; it is a statement about how central Coinbase Ventures is to US crypto deal flow, and it is the reason a planned Base/Coinbase-ecosystem research pass matters: a large share of what these two funds hold overlaps with what Coinbase itself backs.

More directly: Haun and Paradigm were in the same round seven times — Conduit, Euler Finance, Exponential, Farcaster, Fireblocks, Liquid and TaxBit. The source tier list places one at God-tier and does not mention the other. Whatever separates them, it is demonstrably not deal access; both funds are in the room for the same deals. The difference has to be in price, timing and position size — exactly the axis this database cannot yet measure, because none of these sources publish valuations consistently.

06

The wider landscape, judged on artefacts rather than reputation

A source list ranked roughly twenty funds God-tier to F. Where this research agrees, it agrees on evidence: shipped code, published research, realised exits. Where it disagrees, the disagreement is stated in §7 rather than smoothed over.

Builders

Paradigm · cyber•Fund · Robot Ventures · Framework

Ship the infrastructure they fund. Paradigm wrote Reth and Foundry; cyber•Fund built Lido and P2P.org; Robot Ventures' partners run Gauntlet and built Compound; Framework runs validators and provides liquidity directly.

Research-led

Electric Capital · Bain Capital Crypto · Dragonfly · Maven11 · Lemniscap

Published research that founders actually read — Electric's Developer Report is the most cited artefact in the sector; Bain's Alex Evans publishes real cryptography and mechanism-design work.

Realised distributions

Haun Ventures

Not on the source list. Two verified cash exits — Bridge→Stripe, BVNK→Mastercard — and the only crypto-native fund that grew AUM through the 2025–26 contraction that shrank Paradigm, Pantera and a16z crypto.

Large and late is not a compass, even when the fund itself is competent. a16z crypto raised a $2.2bn fifth fund in H1 2026 — real capital strength — but a fund that size cannot write $3m seed checks without hundreds of positions, so it writes large checks into later, priced rounds by construction. That makes it a strong source of capital and a weak signal of what is cheap early. Founders Fund and Polychain sit in the same category on the source list's own criteria.

07

Semantic Ventures, GnosisVC, cyber•Fund

Most interesting of the three

cyber•Fund

Founded 2014 by Konstantin Lomashuk and Vasiliy Shapovalov — the team behind Lido and P2P.org, not an allocator sitting outside the protocols it funds. Portfolio includes =nil; Foundation, one of the four names §5 of the Hooks & MEV research flagged as genuinely private. Its "cybernetic economy" thesis — ZK, FHE, DeFi, network states — runs directly parallel to the verifiable-compute theme this dashboard already holds a position in via NOCK. $100m venture arm since late 2023, on top of a liquid and strategic book.

Thin but consistent

Semantic Ventures

London, founded ~2017. 34 investments in data and network infrastructure — broader than crypto alone. Selective rather than spray-and-pray, but no public research, no open-source contribution and no realised exit was found, so three of the six evaluation criteria in this research simply do not apply. That is a gap in available evidence, not a negative finding.

Strategic, not financial

GnosisVC

GnosisDAO's $40m venture arm, focused on RWA tokenisation, crypto infrastructure and payments within the Ethereum ecosystem. This is a protocol deploying capital to strengthen itself, not a fund optimising for return — informative as a map of where Gnosis wants the ecosystem to go, weak as a signal of where the best risk-adjusted return sits. The same limitation applies to Coinbase Ventures on the Base research.

08

What this research still cannot say

The question this whole line of research exists to answer — does this fund get in cheap, and does the token survive the fund's own unlock — is not yet answered. Two things are missing, and both are buildable from data already in hand:

Valuations. Round size is not entry price; without the pre-money valuation attached to each round, "leads 47% of its rounds" says nothing about whether Paradigm paid a fair price for that lead position. Some crypto-fundraising.info pages carry a valuation field that this pass did not parse.

Unlock behaviour. The most valuable follow-on question in this entire project: take the twenty largest 2024–2026 token generation events, identify the lead investor from this database, and measure the 30/90/180-day price move after each cliff. That turns "Fund X has a bad reputation for dumping" from an opinion into a number, and it is the direct bridge to the float-rule work already live on the Assets tab — Nockchain and Lighter both carry named lead investors in this database today.

What "complete" means here. This database is complete within crypto-fundraising.info at 92% coverage, not complete in the world. Unannounced pre-seed rounds, press releases that name only "the lead and others," secondary token purchases and any fund's public-market positions are invisible to every source tested in this research, not only this one.

Sources & method

Round data: crypto-fundraising.info, scraped 13 August 2026 — 5,868 of 6,407 rounds recovered (92%), 7,655 distinct investors indexed. Fund identifiers are crypto-fundraising.info slugs, not ticker symbols. Amounts are published round sizes; no fund ticket size is disclosed by any public source used here.

Qualitative fund assessment: public reporting on Haun Ventures' AUM and exits (Decrypt, The Block, TheNextWeb, May 2026); Paradigm team and tooling pages; Electric Capital's Developer Report; Bain Capital Crypto team pages; cyber•Fund, Semantic Ventures and GnosisVC public materials. Full source notes in research/crypto-vc-research.md, crypto-vc-haun-ventures.md and crypto-vc-paradigm.md.

Not investment advice. Fund returns are not public and nothing here estimates them. This is a database of publicly disclosed funding rounds and a qualitative assessment built from verifiable artefacts — shipped code, published research, realised acquisitions — not a performance ranking. Round data has known coverage gaps stated throughout. Verify independently before acting on anything here.
